Films arrive as particulars and are laid out as frames. State the six, and the record is lit.

The record is laid out the way the class prints a film: title, director, production company and release year, each followed by its label. The stamps are the class's own questions, answered by its own rules: recent means a release year after 2020, and a bathroom break means a duration over 180 minutes. Try a year of 2021, or a duration of 200, and watch the stamps change.

Every new set of particulars lights a new frame. Nothing is fetched from anywhere; the record says only what it was handed.

How the class keeps its records

The Movies class in movie-metadata.py stores six fields in order: Title, Director, Production_Company, Actors, Duration and Release_Year. The constructor checks the record before storing it: a title must be a non-empty string, actors must be a name or a list of names, and duration and year must each convert to a number. Values are stored as handed in, not converted.

Its questions are isRecent(), requiresBathroomBreak() and workedOnBy(name), which answers for the director or any actor, matching whole names only. Run the demo with python3 movie-metadata.py; its verified output is printed in the repo README, and 18 tests in test_movies.py cover the constructor checks and the method boundaries.
